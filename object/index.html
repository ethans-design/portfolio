Technology Tinder

complete

Technology Tinder is a warm-up in judging technology the way you'd swipe on a dating app — sizing something up on looks and specs, then deciding whether you'd actually take it home. I matched two generations of the same car against each other: the brand-new 2026 Honda Prelude and the 1998 Prelude that first earned the name. For each I built an honest pros-and-cons list from specs, reviews, and what the car is like to live with, then made the call.

Prelude

2026

fig. 1 — Honda's revived halo coupe: sharp styling, screens everywhere, and a hybrid drivetrain. The swipe-right bait.

Pros

  • Looks pretty cool
  • Decent handling
  • Good brakes
  • CarPlay
  • Massive screen

Cons

  • CVT (only option)
  • Slow
  • $42,000
  • Fake driving feel
  • Heavy
  • FWD
  • Hybrid (but bad)

Prelude

1998

fig. 2 — The fifth-gen Prelude, the name's high-water mark: VTEC, four-wheel steering, and a manual. Older, cheaper, and the one you'd actually want.

Pros

  • Looks even cooler
  • Great handling
  • 4-wheel steering
  • Manual or automatic
  • Reliable
  • Incredibly fun to drive
  • VTEC
  • $13,000 new (~$35,000 today)

Cons

  • Also slow (same speed as the new one)
  • ~25 years old
  • FWD

the verdict

This was a gut-check on the assumption that newer is better. The 2026 wins on screens, CarPlay, brakes, and curb appeal — but it's a heavy, front-wheel-drive hybrid locked to a CVT that only simulates the feeling of driving. The 1998 is slower on paper and a quarter-century old, yet it's the one I'd take home: four-wheel steering, VTEC, a real manual, genuinely fun, reliable, and a fraction of the price. Swipe left on the 2026 — swipe right on the '98.
